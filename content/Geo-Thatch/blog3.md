# What I worked on
I created the routes for listing, funding (not funding-drafts), reviews, country, user, and cities. This includes several of each CRUD operation, with listing, funding, and reviews having all 4. I also created the majority of the REST API matrix which plans out the types of routes necessary based on the personas and wireframes. After this, I began to create the mock data using mockaroo and claude. I started with mockaroo, creating the value types and columns I needed, and used claude to adjust as needed (make sure city_name matches the country it is associated with). After finalizing the data, I used a CSV to SQL converter to insert into our databases. This process went much faster than I expected, especially with the tools I used along the way. With streamlit, I reorganized the files (renaming, rerouting the connections, renaming the buttons) and worked on and finished the student listings screen as well as the reviews screen. These both took some time, as I had to become familiar with streamlit and its formatting, but after a couple of hours I got the hang of it and was able to speed up the process. I also worked significantly on the team-blog post, contributing most of the CS side while Maira worked on the other front-end pages. After all of the work I did this week, I feel much more confident going into phase IV, because I feel that we as a team made significant progress towards our end goal.

# What I saw
This week I enjoyed our trip to Strasbourg and Luxembourg. I found the architecture in Strasbourg very interesting, especially the Grande-île de Strasbourg with the 14th and 15th century buildings. On the first day in Strasbourg, we went to Holly’s Diner, which was a 50s themed diner with checkered floors, colorful booths and walls and American posters, depicting Marilyn Monroe, Martin Luther King Jr., and the moon landing. The waitresses were all wearing roller skates, which definitely added to the experience. Overall, a very impressive American experience in France. I also enjoyed the virtual tour in Ghent, and thought that the details in the Lamb of God were super cool. I liked seeing the recovery of the painting, and how many of the original details were able to be uncovered. The cathedral was beautiful, and I felt like I learned a lot about the painting before I saw it, which definitely enhanced the experience. Though, it did make me sad when they showed us the virtual tour guide’s tomb, because I was not aware that he was dead.
